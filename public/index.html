How to play

The classic game

The board is a grid of words. Some belong to your team, some to the other teams, some to nobody, and one is the assassin. Only the spymasters can see which is which.

On your turn, your spymaster gives one clue and a number: a single word that links several of your team’s cards, and how many cards it points to. “Ocean 3” says three of your words relate to the ocean.

Operatives talk it over, tap a card to point at it, then press Reveal. A card of your own colour lets you keep going — up to the clue’s number plus one. Anyone else’s card or a bystander ends your turn. You can also stop early.

First team to uncover all of its cards wins. Reveal the assassin and your team is out on the spot.

Short on players?

You can play for more than one team in the same role. A spymaster can run two teams at once (they already see every card), and an operative can guess for more than one team. Once you’re seated, other teams show Also join next to the seat you can take. You can’t be a spymaster on one team and an operative on another.

Clue etiquette

A clue is about meaning, not spelling or position. It can’t be a word that is still face-down on the board. A clue of 0 means “none of ours relate to this”; ∞ means “I’m catching up on old clues”. Both allow unlimited guesses.

Co-op

Two sides, one board, two different keys. Each side can see nine agents the other side needs to find, and three assassins the other side must avoid. Sides take turns giving clues. Find all fifteen agents before the turns run out and everybody wins. When the turns are gone it is sudden death: no more clues, and one wrong card ends it.

Playing on a call

The rows and columns are labelled, so you can say “I like C4” without reading the word out loud. Spymasters: keep a straight face.
